Approach

Machine learning is the easy part. Operating it is the work.

Most of our engineering time goes into the parts of a deployment that a benchmark score never captures.

Grounded in physics, not just data

Every model is validated against the physical constraints of the equipment it runs on — tolerances, latency budgets, failure modes — before it's judged on accuracy.

Tested under production conditions

Systems run in shadow mode alongside existing processes, under real load and real noise, before they're given control of anything.

Built with the people who run the equipment

Operators and technicians shape the interface and the failure behavior. A model nobody trusts doesn't get used, however accurate it is.

Process

From instrumented site to operating system

The same four stages, regardless of industry. We don't hand off a model and leave — every deployment stays on our on-call rotation.

01

Characterize

We instrument the site or line, collect baseline data across normal and edge-case conditions, and agree on what "working" means with your operators.

02

Model

Models are trained and validated against the physical constraints of the equipment, not just held-out accuracy — latency, failure cost, and drift are all part of the acceptance criteria.

03

Pilot

The system runs in shadow mode alongside current operations for a fixed window, with every disagreement between model and process reviewed by hand.

04

Operate

Full deployment with monitoring dashboards, scheduled retraining, and an engineer on call for the equipment — the same team that built it.
